Latency optimization audit
==========================

Objective: reduce all measured latency paths until further gains require a
correctness, resource, behavior, or compatibility compromise. This audit is
ongoing; the completed work below does not establish that the objective is met.

Preserve cell rendering, Unicode fallback, selection and IME behavior; live
session ownership and explicit legacy rollback; desktop integration; calling
shell configuration; and bounded CPU, memory, queues, and output batching.
Do not remove these costs merely to improve a benchmark number.

Measurement entry points
------------------------
manage.py latency-visible: fresh/additional window mapping and visible readiness,
plus real XTest key events through a PTY to observed pixels. Snapshot/compare
alternates matched workloads. Private Xvfb excludes desktop compositor, physical
keyboard, and monitor scanout; observation adds a polling/readback allowance.

manage.py frame-perf: ordinary paced PTY output to a complete service frame,
without keyboard-response boosting. Report latency, delivered frame count,
daemon CPU counter resolution, and RSS/PSS; compare a frozen daemon snapshot.

manage.py preview-perf: fresh launch/interactive-shell/input timings and
helper-inclusive memory. check-preview also times dense full/row painting.

Remaining audit scope includes GTK presentation after service delivery, dense
painting, view/tab switching, scrolling, and fresh GTK/shell startup. Compare
CPU/memory and validate visual/input/session invariants before accepting changes.

Verified checkpoint: output scheduling
-------------------------------------
The service used a fresh 16 ms sleep even when its existing deadline was near.
It now waits only the remaining interval; both the wait and emission condition
use the same policy. The configured batching limit and input boost are unchanged.

A 40-response native-protocol comparison measured 7.93/16.30 ms median/p95
before and 1.96/2.37 ms after. All responses arrived in both runs. Delivered frames
increased from 61 to 81 because fewer intermediate states were coalesced by the
oversleep; the rate cap was not raised. Daemon PSS measured 253/249 KiB and
CPU below the 10 ms accounting resolution in this workload, rather than a claim
of literally zero processing cost. Frozen samples remain under
.checks/frame-deadline-verified-{baseline,candidate}.json.

Faster delivery exposed early-output correctness failures. Pending-wrap cursor
mapping across width changes and pending-wrap preservation on unchanged-width
resizes are now covered by native regressions. The existing counterfactual proof
command verifies that restoring the old mapping fails. Local and SSH GTK checks
pass with corrected cursor handling and the new output deadline policy.

Follow-up: visible launch/input comparison is not yet conclusive
---------------------------------------------------------------
Ten alternating pairs with twenty keystrokes each measured candidate/baseline
typing medians of 3.80/3.97 ms and p95 of 11.65/8.63 ms. Additional-window visible
readiness medians were 51.78/35.60 ms, with a +25.42 ms paired median difference.
Fresh-window paired readiness was -17.90 ms despite higher candidate marginal
medians, demonstrating substantial variation within the run. These results do
not establish an overall launch/input improvement or rule out a presentation
regression. Investigate this before declaring the full objective complete.
Frozen data: .checks/latency-goal-post-schedule.json. The installed build has not
been replaced by this checkpoint, and live daemons have not been restarted.

Follow-up control and renderer checkpoint
-----------------------------------------
Twenty identical-binary pairs produced additional-window readiness medians of
9.19/24.38 ms, yet their paired median difference was only -1.65 ms. Marginal
medians alone are therefore unreliable for this frame-clock-sensitive probe.
Frozen data: .checks/latency-aa-control-comparison.json.

A quieter twenty-pair repeat against the pre-scheduler build measured a paired
additional-window difference of +1.78 ms, rather than the earlier +25.42 ms.
Fresh readiness remained +9.79 ms in paired samples. The large additional-window
regression did not reproduce; fresh presentation still needs investigation.
Frozen data: .checks/latency-scheduler-repeat.json.

GTK backgrounds now combine consecutive equal-color cells into runs on aligned
device grids. Fractional transforms retain scalar fills to preserve antialias
and alpha-edge behavior. Color/cursor decisions share one policy, and every
background is still painted before any glyph ink. No pixel buffer/cache is added.

Exact scalar-reference comparisons pass for mixed colors, reverse cells,
transparency, clipping, integer scaling, and fractional transforms. Existing
clipped-glyph, selection, IME, ownership, browser and SSH integration checks pass.
Sixty alternating same-process measurements reported scalar/spans background
means of 3.28/0.41 ms in one run and 12.62/1.90 ms under heavier load in another.
This establishes lower background work, not a claimed 7x whole-frame speedup.

Launch-focused follow-up
------------------------
Deferring the empty tab strip's Pango layout until its first catalog refresh
removes unnecessary pre-map setup. Twenty alternating candidate/baseline pairs
measured fresh readiness medians of 99.28/101.20 ms, paired median -2.41 ms;
mapping paired median was -2.57 ms. Additional-window paired readiness was
-0.10 ms. Frozen data: .checks/launch-lazy-tabs-experiment.json. Local and SSH
integration checks pass, including an assertion that mapping an empty window
has not initialized its tab layout.

An experiment painting the first complete frame synchronously showed no gain
(fresh readiness paired +0.38 ms, additional +0.13 ms) and was discarded.
Frozen data: .checks/launch-first-frame-experiment.json.
